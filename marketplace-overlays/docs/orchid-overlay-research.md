# Living Phalaenopsis reference

The overlay models a moth orchid rather than a generic radial flower.

- Phalaenopsis is primarily epiphytic. Its thick persistent leaves and aerial roots reflect that growth habit. Source: Kew Plants of the World Online, `https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:30326-1/general-information`.
- Flowers grow on an elongated raceme that is usually upright or arching. Each flower has three sepals, two lateral petals, a modified lip or labellum, and a central fused reproductive column. Source: American Orchid Society, `https://www.aos.org/orchids/articles/orchid-parts-and-why-they-matter-part-2-the-flower`.
- Phalaenopsis flowers are commonly described as firm, crystalline or waxy, with well-spaced presentation along the inflorescence. The renderer uses translucent gradients, veins, a distinct lip, and a highlighted column to suggest that material quality.

The eight-hour growth and replacement cycle is an intentional Ohverlay time compression. It is a product behavior rather than a botanical claim.
